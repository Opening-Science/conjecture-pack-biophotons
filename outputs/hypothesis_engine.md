# The hypothesis engine

Open AI hypothesis-generation engines, run over this field's own literature, answering the seven questions the field says it cannot answer — and then tested, claim by claim, against the corpus they cite.

**The pipeline.** Each of the seven open questions becomes a structured seed: the question in the field's own words, the verbatim evidence sentences behind it, the registry claims already at stake, and fixed corpus entry points. Every engine receives the identical prompt and reads the literature only through one read-only corpus interface, with no web access, so what differs between engines is architecture and model family rather than prompt or retrieval luck. Their output lands in one schema (the Hypothesis Output Format), is clustered across engines with provenance kept, and is then audited in the certify-or-decline discipline of [theoria](https://github.com/zaladbar/theoria): the reasoning is decomposed into steps, every citation step must resolve to a work that exists in this corpus and actually says what the step claims, and one failed load-bearing step declines the whole hypothesis.

**Nothing here is a result about biology.** These are proposals for experiments, generated by machines and filtered for groundedness. A certified hypothesis is one whose reasoning survived audit, not one that is true.

## Reproducing these numbers

Every figure above is read from `state/audit.db` at render time. To recompute:

```sql
-- certified, not a restatement, by engine
SELECT m.engine, COUNT(DISTINCT c.candidate_id) AS novelty
FROM candidates c JOIN members m USING(candidate_id)
WHERE c.certified=1 AND c.is_restatement=0
GROUP BY m.engine;
```
